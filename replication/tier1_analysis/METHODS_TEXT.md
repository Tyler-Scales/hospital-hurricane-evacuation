# Methods text (verbatim from the DMPHP manuscript, final release 2026-09-10, after source verification)

## Methods

### Design and setting

Retrospective cohort of hospital–storm pairs, one row per hospital per storm, across 21 US Atlantic and Gulf states and the District of Columbia, 1990–2025. Storms are all Atlantic tropical and subtropical cyclones that brought tropical-storm-force winds or a 48-hour forecast threat to a study hospital. A hospital threatened repeatedly contributes one pair per storm (median 22), and 43 of the 156 hospitals with documented decisions appear in more than one storm, so decisions are not independent.

### Hospital roster

Acute-care hospitals came from Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) Provider of Services (POS) files in the National Bureau of Economic Research archive (file years 1990, 1995, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2018). Because POS files retain closed hospitals, each hospital's open period ran from its Medicare participation date to any termination date in the most recent file, and it counted for a storm only within that period. Hospitals open in the 2018 file were carried forward to 2019–2025 storms, so those certified, closed, merged, or resized after 2018 are misrepresented; a 2004–2018 restriction is reported as a sensitivity analysis.

The population is provider category 01 with subtype 01 (short-term acute) or 11 (critical access). Critical access hospitals are included: more than 1,300 rural hospitals have converted since the Balanced Budget Act of 1997⁸ created the program,⁹ each receiving a new CMS Certification Number (CCN). Psychiatric, rehabilitation, long-term acute care, outpatient, and subunit records are excluded; federal facilities were retained. Coordinates came from POS street addresses (US Census geocoder), then OpenStreetMap facility-name lookup, then the ZIP-code center; elevation, surge, and flood-zone values were assigned only to street- or name-level points.

### Storm hazard and cohort inclusion

Storm tracks, intensities, and 34/50/64-knot wind radii came from the National Hurricane Center (NHC) Atlantic database, HURDAT2.¹⁰ Wind-field membership was computed hourly, with quadrant radii interpolated between six-hourly fixes. Fixes before a system was first designated a tropical or subtropical cyclone were excluded; post-tropical fixes (Sandy) were retained.

Candidate pairs were limited to hospitals within 400 nautical miles of the track at closest approach. A pair entered the cohort if the hospital was inside the storm's observed tropical-storm-force (34-knot) wind field, or inside a forecast threat area 48 hours before those winds arrived (the decision point defined below). The threat area adds a 100-nautical-mile wind-field allowance to the era's typical 48-hour track error, so it is wider than the NHC cone; keeping hospitals forecast to be struck but missed avoids judging decisions by hindsight. Because wind radii are recorded only from 2004, the analytic period is 2004–2025; 1990–2003 pairs are described, and adding them to the models is reported as a sensitivity analysis.

### Decision-time information set

The decision clock was anchored to the onset of tropical-storm-force (34-knot) winds at each facility, not to landfall, because patient movement must finish before winds arrive; some emergency medical services agencies stop running ambulances at sustained winds near 40 mph.¹¹ The 48-hour point was used because it is the longest lead available for every study year (official forecasts extended only to 72 hours before 2001) and is a late common decision point, not the earliest. For each pair the analysis used the last official NHC forecast issued at or before 48 hours before winds reached the facility (93% of documented pairs), otherwise 48 hours before closest approach, and computed the hospital's shortest distance to the forecast positions through 72 hours and the highest forecast intensity. The reference point is chosen relative to observed wind onset, so it is a standardized retrospective exposure, not the forecast in hand when each hospital decided.

Track error was measured by comparing 74,084 archived forecasts with actual positions, and the errors by era define the threat area (Supplementary Figure S1); each era's error includes later years within the era, so this calibration is retrospective and differs from the cone NHC published at the time.

### Facility characteristics

Storm-surge inundation depth came from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) Sea, Lake, and Overland Surges from Hurricanes (SLOSH) Maximum-of-Maximums maps¹² at high tide; Category 3 was chosen in advance as a common planning scenario. NOAA codes levee-protected locations distinctly; they were kept as a separate group, not counted as not inundated. Ground elevation came from the US Geological Survey (USGS) 3D Elevation Program, flood zone from the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) National Flood Hazard Layer, and storm-total rainfall from Parameter-elevation Regressions on Independent Slopes Model (PRISM) 4-km daily grids¹³ summed over each storm's passage. Building age was average age of plant (accumulated depreciation divided by annual depreciation expense) from CMS Healthcare Cost Report Information System (HCRIS) cost reports. Beds, ownership, and critical-access status came from the POS file nearest each storm year. Storm intensity at closest approach is the storm's maximum sustained wind, a storm-level measure, not the wind at the hospital; all location measures belong to the certification number's address.

### Registry construction and linkage

Large language model agents (Claude, Anthropic), working from a written codebook, extracted decisions from news archives, state after-action reports, agency communications, and published case studies, one pass per group of storms in August 2026, with every event carrying a source citation and a confidence grade (search procedure, eligibility, schema, and adjudication in the Supplementary Methods). A second reader, a different large language model working from the narratives alone, assessed the 59 FSIP narratives in the penultimate release against the definition. Events were coded as complete pre-landfall evacuation, partial pre-landfall evacuation, documented shelter-in-place, failed shelter-in-place, or unclear; for FSIP events the condition that forced the evacuation was recorded. Documented shelter-in-place required a source stating that the hospital kept operating, or did not move patients, through the storm's passage and immediate aftermath; it certifies success only through the observation time the source reports, and no follow-up window was imposed. Absence of reporting was never coded as sheltering; events coded unclear were re-searched before analysis. For every FSIP event the date forced patient movement began was adjudicated by hand from the sources, with a best estimate, bounds, and a precision grade. Media reports have a recognized surveillance role: the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has used them to track hurricane deaths.¹⁴

Each event was matched to a CCN by approximate name matching within the state against every name the CCN has carried, preferring the certification active in the storm year and requiring city agreement; uncertain matches were reviewed by the authors. Where campuses share one certification, failed sheltering at any campus took precedence and a mixed pattern was coded partial evacuation. Because location measures belong to the certification's address, the failed campus can differ from the modeled site; pairs whose certification carried more than one registry facility in a storm were excluded in a sensitivity analysis.

### Outcomes and analysis

The primary outcome, failed shelter-in-place (FSIP), is a hospital that sheltered and then had to evacuate, completely or partially, after storm conditions reached it because, according to the sources, operations broke down from storm or infrastructure damage such as loss of power, water, or structural integrity. Storm conditions can precede landfall; no time limit was imposed, and the time from landfall to evacuation is reported. FSIP is a working definition; no standard definition of sheltering failure was found. It marks a facility outcome, not a judgment that the decision was wrong. Unlike patient deaths, FSIP can be identified from public sources.

Two logistic regression models were fit: one for the odds of evacuating before landfall, from the decision-time forecast and facility characteristics, and one for the odds of FSIP among sheltering hospitals, from what the storm did and the facility's physical vulnerability. Continuous variables were divided by their standard deviation in the primary sample (0.930 on the log(1+beds) scale, so one unit is about a 2.5-fold difference in beds; 26.8 knots for storm intensity), and the same divisors were used in every subset. Levee-protected locations were analyzed as their own group, never combined with the surge-zone comparison. Because the same hospitals and storms recur, standard errors adjusted for repeated hospitals (80 clusters) and, separately, for repeated storms (23 clusters) are reported; the two adjustments were not combined. Alternative analyses removed Hurricane Katrina, removed each storm in turn, kept only high- and medium-confidence events, added for-profit ownership, added the 1990–2003 pairs, excluded multi-campus certifications, restricted to 2004–2018, excluded events the second reader flagged as ambiguous, and applied Firth's penalized likelihood¹⁵ because the models have 6 to 10 failures per variable (implementation and interval method in the Supplementary Methods). Pairs with unknown flood zone or elevation were dropped from comparisons that use them. The alternative analyses test one result and are not separate hypotheses, so no multiple-comparison adjustment was applied. Analyses used Python 3.9 with statsmodels 0.14.

Two internal audits corrected data errors before final analysis (closed hospitals in the denominator, mislinked registry events, a coordinate fallback, six-hourly wind-field testing). A second large language model, given only the data files and Methods text of the penultimate release, rebuilt the cohort, reproduced the reference output exactly, and found three script-versus-Methods discrepancies (analysis period, penalization, handling of unknown flood zone and elevation), all corrected; its review of the final draft identified the evacuation-date error this version corrects. The final release accompanies the paper but has not itself been independently re-run.

Every registry record underlying the primary analysis was then checked against its cited sources: all 37 failure rows, the 3 auditable re-review additions, and all 67 sheltering records. Each link was retrieved, dead links retried through the Internet Archive, and replacements sought where a source was irrecoverable. The check asked whether a source establishes that the hospital did not evacuate before landfall and, for failures, that patients moved after storm conditions arrived, the mechanism, and the date; for sheltering records it recorded the latest date a source shows the hospital operating without a forced transfer. It was run by a large language model with web access, not a second human abstractor; the three labels it did not support were corrected before analysis.

**Supplementary Methods.** Registry abstraction, second-reader check, and date adjudication

*Tools and dates.* Events were abstracted by large language model agents (Claude, Anthropic) run through Claude Code in ten parallel passes on August 14, 2026, each covering a group of storms; events coded unclear were re-searched on September 7, 2026. Search queries and stopping points were not logged, a limitation; the 95 storm-level official counts recorded alongside the events (for example, Florida's regulator count of hospitals evacuated for Milton) serve as the ascertainment benchmark, and the first pass recovered about 38% of Milton's hospital evacuations against that benchmark.

*Sources and eligibility.* Agents searched the open web: news archives, state emergency-management and health-department after-action reports, federal agency communications, hospital and health-system statements, peer-reviewed case studies, congressional testimony, and court records. Paywalled news databases were not used. An eligible source named a specific hospital and described its status during a named storm; social media posts and anonymous compilations were not eligible. Every event carries at least one source URL.

*Extraction schema.* storm; year; hospital (as named in the source); city; state; action (PRE_FULL, complete pre-landfall evacuation; PRE_PARTIAL, some units moved before landfall; SIP_OK, sheltered and documented as riding out the storm without forced patient movement; POST_FSIP, sheltered and then forced to move patients after storm conditions arrived because operations broke down; POST_REEVAC, evacuated again after a pre-landfall evacuation; UNCLEAR); timing (free text); patients moved; deaths documented; detail (free text); confidence (HIGH, multiple concordant sources or an official record; MEDIUM, a single reliable source or minor discrepancies; LOW, indirect or compilation sources); sources.

*Coding rules.* Partial evacuation counts as evacuation when inpatients were transferred to another facility; discharges home do not count as transfers. Planned transfers completed before storm conditions arrived are pre-landfall evacuation even when the facility later sheltered its remaining patients; a forced post-impact transfer after partial pre-landfall movement is FSIP. An announced evacuation that moved some patients before being reversed is FSIP (partial, aborted) and is flagged. Regional utility loss (city water or power) that forced patient movement is FSIP; the label describes the facility outcome, not fault. Where the mechanism was not documented the event is retained with its confidence grade and the gap is noted in the detail field. Absence of reporting was never coded as sheltering.

*Linkage and adjudication.* Each event was matched to a CMS Certification Number by token overlap, sequence similarity, and city agreement against every name the CCN has carried, preferring the certification active in the storm year. Matches scoring at least 0.80 with city and active-year agreement were accepted automatically (188); matches scoring 0.55–0.80, failing a check, or later found wrong on audit were reviewed with web verification and recorded with a reason (68); 5 events could not be linked. Twenty-five manual link overrides and every adjudication verdict are in the accompanying files.

*Second-reader check.* A different large language model (OpenAI ChatGPT, Astra 6.0), given the registry narratives and data files of the penultimate release, assessed all 59 FSIP events then in the registry against the definition (the 4 FSIP labels added afterwards by the source re-review, Homestead, Lane Regional, Select Specialty, and Emerald Coast Behavioral, were not covered): 44 consistent on the narrative; 4 outside the acute-care population and 2 outside the study states, all already excluded from the cohort; 2 with partial pre-landfall movement followed by FSIP, consistent under the rules above; and 7 flagged for ambiguity of mechanism, patient movement, or timing (Tulane-Lakeside, Hancock, Mainland, Bayonet Point, Ben Taub, DeTar, East Houston). Bayonet Point and East Houston were subsequently recoded as pre-landfall evacuations by the source verification described below, and Tulane-Lakeside and Mainland are levee-protected and outside the primary model; the 3 remaining flagged events in the primary sample are removed in a sensitivity analysis (Table 4). The check was narrative-based with limited online spot-checking; it tests whether each narrative meets the definition, not whether the cited sources support the narrative. The same reader showed that the earlier automated date extraction had used reopening dates for four Katrina events and flagged a fifth (Select Specialty) for source review, which prompted the manual date adjudication below.

*Firth implementation.* Jeffreys-prior penalty implemented from the published equations¹⁵ with Newton–Raphson iteration on the modified score, step halving on the penalized objective, a convergence tolerance of 10⁻⁸, and an explicit convergence check (7 iterations for the primary model). Confidence intervals are Wald intervals from the inverse Fisher information evaluated at the penalized estimates, the convention of the logistf implementation; they are not derived from the Hessian of the penalized objective (which gives 0.63–4.06 rather than 0.62–4.13 for the surge odds ratio) and are not profile penalized-likelihood intervals. Clustered standard errors use 81 hospital clusters or 24 storm clusters, fitted separately.

*Source verification.* After the analyses above were fixed, every registry record underlying the primary analysis was checked against its cited sources by a large language model with web access (Claude, Anthropic, September 9, 2026): all 37 rows behind the failure events, the 3 auditable events added by the re-review, and all 67 documented shelter-in-place records. Of 391 cited links, 296 resolved, 58 were recovered from the Internet Archive, and 37 could not be retrieved; a replacement or corroborating source was found for 85 records and added to the registry. Three failure labels were not supported and were corrected: DeSoto Memorial Hospital (Charley 2004), whose only source states the hospital provided uninterrupted care and moved patients within the building, recoded to documented shelter-in-place; Regional Medical Center Bayonet Point (Hermine 2016), fully evacuated about a day before landfall with a census of zero at landfall, and East Houston Regional Medical Center (Harvey 2017), evacuated the morning before landfall, both recoded to complete pre-landfall evacuation. Two adjudicated dates were moved earlier to match the earliest documented movement. Two events where the evidence conflicts (Lee Memorial in Ian 2022 and Emerald Coast Behavioral in Michael 2018) and three that turn on whether intra-facility relocation, a transfer eleven days after landfall, or emergency-department-only operation meet the definitions are identified in the accompanying file and were left as coded. For each shelter-in-place record the check also recorded the latest date a source shows the hospital operating without a forced transfer: a date was established for 64 of 67, of which 57 rest on contemporaneous reporting at a median of 3 days after landfall (interquartile range 1 to 6 days) and 7 on a later retrospective attestation. The check is a source-level audit by a language model, not a second human abstractor, and does not establish that every event is correctly coded.

*Date adjudication.* For every FSIP event the date forced patient movement began was set by hand from the timing and detail narratives, recording a best estimate, lower and upper bounds, the completion date where known, a precision grade, and the narrative basis. Exact (31): a stated date of patient movement. Approximate (19): movement placed within about a day by the narrative ("the day after landfall", "the weekend of August 26–27"). Bounded (13): the narrative gives only a window ("in the days after", "early October"); the best estimate is the window midpoint (rounded down for a half day) and the bounds are reported. Each bounded window is classified in the data file as stated by a source (1 event), partly stated (6; one endpoint from a source or from the host hospital's dates), or inferred from regional context (6; for example, New Orleans hospitals whose sources say only that evacuation followed the storm, bounded by the day after landfall and the completion of the regional hospital evacuation on September 2). Inferred bounds are assumptions, not source-established endpoints. Reopening dates, report dates, and article dates were never used as evacuation dates. Landfall is the storm's HURDAT2 landfall record nearest the hospital among those on or before the upper bound of the evacuation start (UTC dates), or the nearest landfall overall when the failure preceded every landfall.
